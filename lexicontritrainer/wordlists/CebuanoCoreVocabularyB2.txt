# CEBUANO VOCABULARY — UNIFIED CATEGORY-ORGANIZED DECK
# Format: word<w>root<r>Cebuano definition<d>english, translations<t>
#         The <r> root tag is OMITTED when the word is its own root (or has no root).
#         Roots group words that are naturally learnt together regardless of word class:
#         a noun and all the verbs derived from it share one root. Etymology alone is not
#         enough — words whose modern meaning has drifted from the root stand on their own.
#         385 of 1777 entries carry a root tag; 1392 are their own roots.
#         TRUE HOMOGRAPHS — words that share a form but belong to different groups —
#         get a parenthetical disambiguator in the root tag, e.g. basa(read) vs basa(wet),
#         so basahon "book" unambiguously joins the "read" group. Senses that are one
#         lexeme (a verb and its noun: buhat "to do" / buhat "deed") keep a single
#         undisambiguated root, because they are correctly learnt together.
# All entries pure Cebuano (no Tagalog); definitions use only native Cebuano vocabulary
# (no Spanish/English loanwords or their derivatives).
# Organized first by word class (verbs, adjectives, nouns, function words), then by
# semantic category.
# Connectors (C1-C2 sentence-level) and the standard grammar particles/pronouns are
# kept in their own files. See also cebuano_connectors_grammar_organized.txt for a
# thematically-organized view of all those grammar words.
#
# Total entries: 1777  (620 verbs + 319 adjectives + 621 nouns + 30 function words + 187 connectors/particles/pronouns)
#         No entry names itself, its root, or any word of its own family in either the
#         definition or the translation: a variant is defined by what it DOES, never as
#         "another form of X".
#         Multi-word headwords are admitted ONLY as grammatical units — conjunctions,
#         prepositions, free-choice indefinites and markers (22 in total). Compositional
#         phrases and idioms are excluded; their parts are entries in their own right.
